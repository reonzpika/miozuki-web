# Should Baroque Pearl Earrings Match? What to Look for in a Pair

<GuideSchema title="Should Baroque Pearl Earrings Match? What to Look for in a Pair" path="/pearl-guide/should-baroque-pearl-earrings-match" updated="2026-11-17" />

Baroque pearl earrings are meant to show the irregular shapes of the pearls. One may be rounder and the other longer or more curved, even in a pair chosen to go together. If you like a coordinated look, I’d compare how big the pearls look side by side and how similar they are in colour and shine.

Shape and condition are two different things. A curved outline or a small ridge can form as a pearl grows. A mark that looks like a chip, a crack or peeling is worth asking about, and a loose post is a problem with the fitting. If you’re shopping from photos, ask for a closer view and have the seller check anything you’re unsure about. ## Checklist for choosing baroque pearl earrings

Put both earrings side by side or open a photo that shows them together. I’d look at them from a normal distance first, then work through these checks.

1. **Expect differences in shape.** One pearl may be rounder or more curved. Look at both together and choose a pair whose shapes feel balanced to you.
2. **Check the size of each pearl.** Compare height and width in millimetres. If one looks larger in the photo, check that both earrings face the same way.
3. **Compare colour and lustre in the same light.** Look for differences in warmth or shine with both pearls facing the same way in soft daylight.
4. **See where each earring will sit.** For studs, ask for a back view to see where the posts sit. For drops, compare the distance from the piercing to the bottom of each pearl.
5. **Check the surface and fittings.** A small ridge or dimple can be part of how the pearl grew. Ask the seller to inspect anything that looks chipped, cracked or peeling, or any post that seems loose. If you’re shopping online, ask for a close-up from another angle.
6. **Confirm which pair you’ll receive.** Ask whether the listing shows your pair. If it shows an example and the differences matter to you, ask for a photo of both earrings together before ordering.

## Is it normal for one pearl to look bigger?

A small difference can be part of a baroque pair. One pearl may be taller while the other is wider, so two pearls with different measurements can still look similar when worn. Look at them together from the front and ask for the height and width of each if one looks noticeably larger. The [pearl earring size guide](/pearl-guide/pearl-earring-size-guide) helps put those measurements in context.

If one stud sits lower, swap the earrings from one ear to the other and look again from straight ahead. If the difference follows the earring, look at the pearl’s shape and where the post sits on the back. If it stays on the same side, the position of your piercings may be part of what you’re seeing. For drops, compare the distance from the piercing to the bottom of each pearl. Two similar pearls can hang at different heights if the fittings differ.

## What if one pearl looks warmer or shinier?

Put the earrings next to each other near a window, facing the same way. A pearl can look warmer or brighter at a different angle to the light. If the difference persists when you turn the pair together, ask yourself whether you like the contrast. For a subtle match, I’d look for a similar overall tone and reflections of similar brightness and sharpness. That quality of reflection is called lustre. ## Are bumps or marks part of the shape?

If you spot a mark in a photo, ask the seller to show that same spot straight on and from the side. A reflection may shift as the angle changes. A mark that stays in the same place deserves a closer look, so ask the seller to describe what they see on the pearl in person.

## When to ask for a photo of the exact pair

If the differences matter to you, ask whether the listing shows the pair you’ll receive. You could say, “Could I see the two earrings together, facing forwards in the same light?” If you’re wondering where each stud will sit on your ear, ask for each pearl’s height and a back view showing where the posts sit.

For drops, ask the seller to measure the distance from the piercing to the bottom of each pearl. That gives you a better idea of how far each one will hang.
