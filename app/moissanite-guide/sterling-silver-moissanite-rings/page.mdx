# Sterling Silver Moissanite Rings: What 925 Silver and White Gold Plating Mean

<GuideSchema title="Sterling Silver Moissanite Rings: What 925 Silver and White Gold Plating Mean" path="/moissanite-guide/sterling-silver-moissanite-rings" updated="2026-11-04" />

Sterling silver moissanite rings have a silver-alloy setting and a moissanite gemstone. When a listing also says “white gold plated”, it describes a finish over the silver. The ring can be both sterling silver and white-gold-plated; the two descriptions refer to different parts of it.

That distinction is useful when two white-metal rings look similar online. The photograph shows you the design, while the material description tells you what you are choosing. Here is how to read those details before exploring the [Miozuki moissanite ring collection](/collections/moissanite-rings).

## The metal and finish at a glance

| Wording in a listing | What it tells you | What to check next |
|---|---|---|
| 925 or S925 sterling silver | The silver alloy is 92.5% silver | Whether it has an additional surface finish |
| White-gold-plated sterling silver | Sterling silver underneath a coating of white gold | The coating specification and care advice |
| Silver plated | Silver is a surface layer over another metal | What the underlying metal is |
| Solid white gold | The ring's main metal is a gold alloy rather than silver with a gold coating | The gold fineness and any surface plating |

## What does 925 silver mean?

The number 925 means 925 parts in a thousand are silver, or 92.5%. The remaining 7.5% consists of other metals in the alloy.

You may see the description written as sterling silver, 925 silver or S925. In this context, they refer to the same silver fineness. The exact composition of the remaining alloy should not be guessed from the number alone.

Sterling silver is real silver alloyed with other metals, rather than pure silver. Calling it an alloy does not make it an imitation. It tells you more precisely what the metal is.

A stamp is useful information, but read it alongside the seller's description. S925 identifies the stated silver fineness; it does not specify the coating or gemstones. It is also different from a full independent hallmark: in the UK, for example, [an assay-office hallmark includes several identifying marks](https://www.theassayoffice.co.uk/our-services/hallmarking/).

## What white gold plating adds

White gold plating is a surface coating of white gold over the underlying metal. On a ring described as white-gold-plated sterling silver, the silver remains the main metal underneath. The finish does not turn it into a solid white gold ring.

The word “plated” also leaves some questions unanswered. It does not, by itself, state the coating's thickness, its gold fineness or how long it will retain its original appearance. If those details matter to your choice, ask about the particular ring rather than treating every plated finish as identical.

I’d keep the full description when comparing options. It is easy to shorten “white-gold-plated sterling silver” to “white gold” in your mind, especially when both photographs have a similar colour. Keeping the silver and the coating together in your notes makes the comparison clearer.

## White gold or rhodium plating: which should you choose?

If you are comparing two S925 rings, one with white gold plating and one with rhodium plating, **both are still sterling silver rings**. The difference is the surface finish. White gold plating uses a gold alloy; rhodium plating uses rhodium, a separate metal. The word “gold” in the coating description does not make the first ring a solid gold purchase.

For shopping, the useful differences are how the finish looks and what upkeep to expect:

| What matters to you | What to compare |
|---|---|
| A bright, cool white appearance | Rhodium is commonly used for a bright, reflective white finish. White gold’s shade varies with its alloy, so ask to see the actual finish on each ring rather than judging from the names. |
| Keeping that appearance with regular wear | Either coating can wear. Ask about coating thickness, care and whether a finish refresh is available, including its cost. The coating name alone cannot tell you which of two rings will keep its finish longer. |
| Understanding a price difference | Compare the whole ring: setting, gemstones, finish specifications and aftercare. White gold plating over S925 is not the same material choice as a solid white gold setting. |

[GIA explains how rhodium gives jewellery a bright white finish](https://4cs.gia.edu/en-us/blog/white-gold-vs-silver/), including its use over silver and solid white gold. That is why two rings with different underlying metals can look similar in a photograph.

I’d compare the appearance of the actual rings first, then ask what looking after each finish involves. If both designs appeal to you, a clear answer about upkeep is more useful than assuming one coating is better because its name sounds more valuable.

## What should you expect as you wear it?

A plated finish can wear with use. How quickly that happens depends on factors including the coating thickness and how the piece is worn. There is no single lifespan to assign to every white-gold-plated silver ring.

Keep the silver setting, surface coating and gemstone separate when thinking about longevity. A durable stone does not make the coating permanent. Equally, a change in the finish does not by itself tell you that the moissanite has changed.

Sterling silver can tarnish and scratch, as [GIA notes in its silver overview](https://4cs.gia.edu/en-us/blog/pick-right-white-metal-engagement-wedding-ring/). Tarnish, scratches and worn plating are different things, so avoid assuming that any change in appearance has the same cause or remedy. For cleaning and storage, follow the [jewellery care guide](/pages/jewellery-care-guide) and advice for your particular finish rather than applying an unplated-silver polishing method to every ring.

Before buying, it is reasonable to ask whether the finish can be refreshed, who would assess the piece and what that would cost. Those are questions to resolve for the actual ring, not a promise of an included replating service. If you are weighing silver against gold for regular wear, the [moissanite ring choosing guide](/moissanite-guide/how-to-choose-a-moissanite-ring) covers the broader material decision.

## A few useful questions before you choose

You do not need a technical vocabulary to get a clear answer. A short message about the ring you like is enough:

> “I’m looking at the sterling-silver version of this ring. Could you confirm the surface finish, any available coating specifications and the care advice for that option?”

Then check the things that matter to your purchase:

1. **Does the selected option match the description?** A page offering both silver and gold needs to be read with your chosen material in mind.
2. **Are the centre and accent stones identified separately?** This matters on pavé, halo and other designs with smaller stones.
3. **Is the finish described clearly?** Ask about ambiguous wording rather than inferring the coating from colour.
4. **What should you expect from upkeep?** Get specific advice for the piece, including whether a finish assessment or refresh is available.
