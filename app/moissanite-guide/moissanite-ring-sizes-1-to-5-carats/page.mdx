I find millimetres more helpful than carat labels when picturing a ring on the hand. As a rough guide, round diamonds from 1 to 5 carats span about 6.5 to 11.1 mm in diameter. The table below gives approximate diameters for each size; the photograph gives you a visual reference for how smaller and larger round diamonds look on a hand. For a moissanite ring, check the centre stone's actual measurements too: its shape and what the seller means by “carats” can change the comparison.

Then think about how much you want to notice the ring when you look down at your hand. Would you enjoy a smaller centre, something more eye-catching, or a ring that becomes part of your whole look? As you browse the [moissanite ring collection](/collections/moissanite-rings), keep two or three favourites in mind. You do not need to choose the largest stone your budget allows.

With the same shape, increasing carat weight does not increase the width at the same rate. That is why I find it easier to compare millimetres than to rely on the carat number alone.

## What do the carat descriptions mean?

[GIA explains that carat measures weight, not size](https://4cs.gia.edu/en-us/diamond-carat-weight/). With moissanite, also check whether the seller means actual gemstone weight, diamond equivalent weight (DEW), or the total weight of stones in the ring. These are different descriptions.

DEW is a comparison with diamond size; it does not mean the moissanite weighs that amount. A total-carats description may include smaller stones, so it should not be treated as the centre stone's weight without checking.

Here are the **approximate round-diamond diameters** behind that size range. Use them to understand a diamond-equivalent description, then check the measurements of the moissanite you are considering. The figures are sourced reference estimates, not measurements taken from the photograph above.

| Round diamond reference | Approximate diameter | A way to approach the choice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 carat | 6.5 mm | Start here if you prefer a smaller centre. |
| 2 carats | 8.2 mm | Compare this with 1 carat if you want the centre to take up more space. |
| 3 carats | 9.4 mm | Consider this part of the range if you enjoy a more prominent centre. |
| 4 carats | 10.3 mm | Look closely at the overall outline and how much attention you want the ring to draw. |
| 5 carats | 11.1 mm | Start here if a large centre is part of what you love about a ring. |

Approximate diameters are from [Diamond & Sapphire's round-diamond reference chart](https://diamondandsapphire.com/pages/diamond-size-chart). Cut proportions affect dimensions. ## How I would choose the amount of presence

Start with the pair you feel most drawn to. If you keep returning to smaller centres, compare the first two sizes. If you love a larger outline, spend time with the upper end of the range instead. There is no need to work through every size before allowing yourself a favourite.

Then look at a photograph that shows the whole hand, not only a close-up of the ring. Compare like with like where you can: the same finger, a similar hand position, the same camera distance and the same image scale. A tightly cropped photograph can make even a smaller centre fill the screen.

I would also picture the ring beside any band you plan to wear with it. If you need help with the rest of the design, our [guide to choosing a moissanite ring](/moissanite-guide/how-to-choose-a-moissanite-ring) brings the shape, setting and metal choices together. For the fit of the band itself, use the [NZ/AU ring size converter](/pages/nz-au-to-us-ring-size-converter); finger size and centre-stone size are separate decisions.

Finally, leave yourself a budget you feel happy with. You can use the [NZ moissanite ring cost guide](/moissanite-guide/moissanite-ring-cost-nz) for context, then return to the ring you would enjoy looking at and wearing. I would choose that feeling over reaching a particular carat number.
