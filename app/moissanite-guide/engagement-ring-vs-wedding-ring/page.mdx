# Engagement Ring vs Wedding Ring: What's the Difference?

<GuideSchema title="Engagement Ring vs Wedding Ring" path="/moissanite-guide/engagement-ring-vs-wedding-ring" updated="2026-10-21" />

I like to start with a simple distinction: an engagement ring marks the intention to marry, while a wedding ring marks the marriage itself. The engagement ring is usually given at a proposal, and the wedding ring is usually exchanged during the ceremony. Whether you choose one ring or both, I believe its meaning comes from the commitment you give it.

I would not let the names decide the design for you. An engagement ring can be a band, and a wedding ring can have gemstones. The more useful question is: do you want one ring to carry both meanings, or a separate piece for each occasion?

## The difference at a glance

| Question | Engagement ring | Wedding ring |
|---|---|---|
| What does it mark? | The intention to marry | The marriage |
| When is it usually given? | At a proposal or during the engagement | During the wedding ceremony |
| Common design | A centre stone, such as a solitaire, or a band | A plain or stone-set band |
| Does it need a diamond? | No. Choose a gemstone or no stones | No. Choose a gemstone or no stones |
| Can it be worn alone? | Yes, including after the wedding | Yes, with or without an engagement ring |
| Is there a required spend? | No. Set a budget that suits you | No. Include it in your total if buying both |

“Wedding band” and “wedding ring” usually mean the same thing. The word band describes the familiar ring shape; it does not mean the ring must be plain or free of gemstones.

These are common customs, rather than a rule for every couple or culture. GIA's [guide to wearing engagement rings and wedding bands](https://4cs.gia.edu/en-us/blog/how-to-wear-engagement-ring-wedding-band/) also describes wearing them together, separately or on different hands.

## Do you want one ring or both?

My starting point is what you want on your hand after the wedding. Picture an ordinary day: would you reach for one familiar ring, a band on its own, or the two together? That is the choice I would make before narrowing down designs.

### One ring for both chapters

If you love a simpler look, I would consider keeping one ring. You can continue wearing your engagement ring after marriage without adding a wedding band, or choose a band from the start to mark both moments. This lets you put your budget into one piece and avoids having to choose a second design around it.

The trade-off is that you will not have a separate ring for each occasion. If that distinction matters to either of you, talk it through before ordering. One ring should feel like a shared choice.

### An engagement ring and a wedding band

I also see the appeal of marking each occasion with a separate piece. Two rings give you the option of a band on its own some days and both rings on others. You can wear them on different hands too.

The extra decisions are the total cost and, if you want to wear them on one finger, how the pair sits together. I would plan for both before spending the whole ring budget on the first piece.

### A wedding ring without an engagement ring

If you would rather choose your rings together after the proposal, you can skip an engagement ring and start with wedding rings. I would consider this if neither of you wants a separate proposal ring. Each partner can choose a different design or number of rings.

## Which ring should you buy first?

If you want both, the engagement ring usually comes first because the proposal comes before the wedding. My advice is to think about the eventual wedding band while choosing that first ring, even if you buy them months apart. You do not need to make every decision at once.

1. **Decide on the overall plan.** One ring, two rings worn together, or two worn separately? Leave room in your total budget for what you actually want.
2. **Choose the first ring with that plan in mind.** For a surprise proposal, find out whether your partner wants a solitaire, a band or a say in the choice. If you are unsure, choosing the ring together after the proposal is an option.
3. **Confirm the fit before ordering.** Use the existing [ring size guide](/pages/size-guide) rather than guessing from another person's ring.
4. **Check the pairing if you want a stack.** Ask the jeweller whether the exact rings leave a gap or touch around the stone setting. You can plan both designs now and buy them at different times. If you will wear them separately, focus on how each ring feels on its own.
5. **Work backwards from your date.** Check the current production and delivery information on each product, especially for made-to-order rings. Allow time to receive and check both pieces before the ceremony.

For the separate questions of stone shape, setting and metal, use our [guide to choosing a moissanite ring](/moissanite-guide/how-to-choose-a-moissanite-ring).
