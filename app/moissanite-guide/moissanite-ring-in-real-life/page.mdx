## Start with how the ring looks on the hand

Think about how you normally see a ring. You glance down while you’re holding a cup or resting your hand on the table. A wider shot gives you something closer to that view. You can see the band running around the finger and decide whether you like the amount of space the centre stone takes up.

The [3 Carat Oval Pavé Ring](/products/3-carat-oval-cut-pave-ring) is the example for this guide. Its oval centre measures 10 × 8.2 mm according to the listing, and small stones line the band. If you’re comparing it with another ring, look up both stones’ measurements and find a whole-hand view of each. The same stone can have a different presence on a different hand, and changing the crop alone can make it seem larger or smaller.

If you’re still deciding how much of your finger you’d like the stone to cover, our [moissanite ring size guide](/moissanite-guide/moissanite-ring-sizes-1-to-5-carats) explains how to use centre-stone measurements when you compare designs.

## Look closely at the stone, then turn the ring to the side

A close-up is where I’d look at how the ring is put together. On the Oval Pavé, follow the edge of the oval and see where the claws hold it. Look at the band where it meets the setting. Those details can explain why two rings with similar centre stones feel quite different to you.

From the side, look at the space between the bottom of the stone and the band. That view helps you see how high the setting sits. If height matters to you, ask for the finished measurement for the ring you’re considering. Our [guide to high and low ring settings](/moissanite-guide/high-set-vs-low-set-moissanite-rings) shows where to look.

## What changes between daylight and indoor light?

To compare lighting for yourself, try to see the same ring in soft window light and under the sort of lamp you use at home. Near a window, you may have an easier time following the outline of the stone and seeing the band as a whole. Under a small, bright light, a slow turn can bring individual flashes into view. Some of those flashes may be coloured. Gemmologists call that colour *fire*.

I’d watch the whole turn if you have a clip. Notice how the ring looks between flashes as well as when a facet catches the light. That gives you a better sense of how lively it might feel when you wear it. The exact effect will vary with the light, angle and stone.

## What to ask for when you’re shopping online

If you’re choosing a moissanite ring in NZ and can’t try it on, browse the [moissanite ring collection](/collections/moissanite-rings) and use the product galleries to compare design details. If you’re unsure how a ring will look at everyday scale, ask for an unedited on-hand photo.

| If you’re wondering… | Ask to see… |
| --- | --- |
| Will it feel subtle or stand out? | A photo with the whole hand in frame and the centre-stone dimensions |
| How high does it sit? | A straight side view and the finished setting height |
| How visible are the coloured flashes in different light? | A slow turn near a window and another under an ordinary indoor light |

The view I’d come back to is the whole hand. If you like the ring there, the close-up and the side view can help you decide whether you like the smaller details too.
