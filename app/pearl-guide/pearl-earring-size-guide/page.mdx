# Pearl Earring Size Guide: What the Measurements Look Like On

I recommend **4 or 6 mm pearl studs** for a subtle look, **8 mm** for a fuller look, or **10 to 12 mm** for a bolder look. How much of your earlobe the pearl covers depends on the size of your lobe and where your piercing sits.

## How round pearl studs look from 4 to 12 mm

A **4 mm** pearl gives you a tiny point of light. At **6 mm**, you can see more of its round shape. An **8 mm** pearl has a fuller look, while **10–12 mm** studs give the pearl a more noticeable presence on the lobe. All five measurements below are the diameter of a round pearl, measured straight across.

| Round pearl size | What you might notice when worn |
|---|---|
| **4 mm** | A tiny accent with plenty of lobe visible around it. Consider this if you like the look of a small stud or want a little pearl in a second piercing. |
| **6 mm** | A small, distinct pearl with an easy-to-see round shape. It can suit you if you like a modest stud that still looks clearly like a pearl. |
| **8 mm** | A fuller circle that stands out more than a 6 mm stud. How much of your lobe it covers depends on your piercing position and lobe size. |
| **10 mm** | A bolder stud that covers more of the lobe. If the post sits behind the centre, a 10 mm pearl reaches about 5 mm below the piercing. Compare that with the distance to the bottom edge of your lobe. |
| **12 mm** | A prominent round stud. It may extend below the edge of your lobe if the lobe is small or the piercing sits low. Look at a photo of the earrings being worn and ask about the backing if you are unsure how they will sit. |

If you are deciding between **6 and 8 mm**, the extra 2 mm adds about 1 mm on either side of the centre. Try drawing both circles and holding them by your ear to see which one looks more like you. For a baroque stud, check its height and width separately because the outline can stretch further in one direction.

## Which measurement are you looking at?

| Measurement | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Round pearl diameter | How wide the pearl is across its face. A 6 mm round pearl measures about 6 mm across. |
| Baroque pearl height and width | How tall the pearl is and how wide it is at its broadest point in the listed orientation. |
| Overall earring height | How much space the visible design takes up from top to bottom. Ask what the seller includes in that measurement. |
| How far a drop hangs | Where its lowest point sits in relation to the piercing. Ask for the distance from the piercing to the bottom if that is the number you need. |

[GIA’s guide to measuring pearls](https://4cs.gia.edu/en-us/blog/pearl-quality-101-gia-examines-classifies-pearls/) explains why round pearls are described by diameter and other shapes are measured in more than one direction. The extra number helps when you are looking at a baroque pair. A tall, narrow pearl and a tall, wide pearl can look quite different even when they are the same height.

## How to picture the size on your own ear

1. **Start with a pair you already wear.** Lay one earring next to a millimetre ruler and measure across the part you see when you wear it. That gives you a familiar size to compare against.
2. **Sketch the shape on paper.** Try a 6 mm circle for a round pearl, or sketch a baroque shape inside a rectangle about 19 mm tall and 14 mm wide. Hold the paper beside your ear in a mirror. It gives you a rough idea of the space the pearl takes up.
3. **Look for the piercing in the photo.** A stud with the post near the top of its design may extend further below the piercing than one with the post nearer the middle. A side view can also show how far the earring sits away from the ear.
4. **Ask for the missing number.** For a drop, ask how far it hangs below the piercing. If weight matters to you, ask for the weight of one earring and compare it with a pair you already enjoy wearing.

If two sizes look similar on a screen, try the paper comparison with both. Look in a mirror from the distance you would normally stand, then see which outline you prefer. The paper gives you a sense of coverage. The photos show the shape and setting, and a try-on is where you can judge the feel for yourself.

## Choose how much pearl you want to see

For a small touch of pearl, begin with a smaller diameter and check whether other stones or metal add height. If you want the pearl to be the first thing you notice, look at its width as well as its height. A wider baroque stud can give you that look while sitting at the lobe. If you want movement below the ear, check where a drop ends and how it moves when worn.
