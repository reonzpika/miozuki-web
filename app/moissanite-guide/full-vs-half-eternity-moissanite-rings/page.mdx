# Full vs Half Eternity Moissanite Rings: Which Should You Choose?

<GuideSchema title="Full vs Half Eternity Moissanite Rings" path="/moissanite-guide/full-vs-half-eternity-moissanite-rings" updated="2026-10-22" />



A full eternity ring has stones all the way around, while a half eternity ring has stones across the top and plain metal underneath. The two differences I would think about first are resizing and comfort: full eternity rings generally cannot be resized through a conventional alteration, while half eternity designs may allow resizing, depending on the metal and construction. Check our [ring sizing guidance](/pages/size-guide) and confirm the options for your chosen ring before ordering.

For everyday comfort, I would lean towards half eternity. A plain underside may feel more comfortable, though the setting, dimensions and fit still matter. To me, full eternity has the prettier, uninterrupted circle of sparkle, but the stones and settings underneath can make it less comfortable. It is a lovely choice if that all-around sparkle matters more to you, provided you are happy with how the exact ring feels on your finger.

If you would like to picture the styles as you read, explore our [moissanite rings collection](/collections/moissanite-rings).

## The difference at a glance

| What matters to you? | Full eternity | Half eternity |
|---|---|---|
| Stone coverage | Around the whole band | Across the upper part of the band |
| When the ring turns | A stone-set section remains in view | The plain section can come into view |
| Under your finger | Stones and their settings continue underneath | A plain metal section sits underneath when centred |
| What to check for comfort | Setting height, edges, width and fit | The same details, including where the stone-set section ends |
| Future size changes | Ask about the exact design before buying | A plain section does not guarantee resizing is available |

I would use this comparison as a starting point, then look closely at the rings themselves. Full and half describe where the stones sit. They do not tell you how large the stones are, how wide the band is or how it will feel.

## Start with the view you want on your hand

### Full eternity: a continuous circle of stones

There is a quiet appeal to a circle of stones with no beginning or end. With full eternity, the pattern continues underneath your finger, so there is no plain back to keep out of view. If the ring turns, you still see stones, though the light and angle will change their appearance.

I would consider this if the continuous pattern is what draws you to an eternity ring. Just remember to check how those settings feel beneath and between your fingers. Full eternity describes the pattern; it does not stop the ring from turning.

### Half eternity: a row of stones with a plain back

With half eternity, the stones sit across the top and a plain metal section rests beneath your finger. You still have a row of sparkle to look down at. How much of it you see depends on the design, ring size and how the band sits on your hand.

If the ring turns, you may see the end of the row or some plain metal and want to turn it back. I would ask yourself whether that would bother you. It does not mean a half eternity ring turns more often, only that you may notice it more when it does.

“Half” is a design description, rather than a promise that stones cover precisely half the circumference in every size. Check photographs of the actual style.

## Which feels better for regular wear?

I would give comfort just as much thought as the view from above. A plain underside may appeal if you prefer metal beneath your finger, but a half eternity ring still has settings along the top and sides. With full eternity, those settings continue around your finger. Their height and finish matter alongside the band shape and fit.

If you can try the ring, take a little time with it. Let your hand rest naturally, bring your fingers together and gently close your hand. Notice where you feel the band against neighbouring fingers and underneath. If you are choosing online, I would ask for side and underside views and any missing measurements before deciding.

For regular wear, check the construction and care needs of the finished ring as well as how it feels. Our [jewellery care guide](/pages/jewellery-care-guide) covers handling, cleaning and storage.

## Check sizing and resizing before ordering

My advice is to settle the size before ordering, rather than count on changing it later. Start with our [ring size guide](/pages/size-guide), and ask us about the exact design if you are unsure.

A full eternity ring has settings around its circumference, so do not assume there is a plain section available for a conventional size alteration. A half eternity ring has a plain section, but that alone does not establish whether it can be resized safely or whether the seller offers the service. Metal, construction and the amount of change all need assessment.

[GIA's engagement-ring buying advice](https://4cs.gia.edu/en-us/blog/tips-for-buying-an-engagement-ring/) explains that rings with pavé, bead-set stones or other band details require more resizing work than solitaires. That is general design guidance, not confirmation that either Miozuki example is eligible.

Our [size guide](/pages/size-guide) says resizing is not offered for non-gold rings. For a gold eternity ring, confirm the options for that exact design before ordering. A general resizing offer should not replace confirmation for the ring you are buying.

## Wearing it alone or beside another ring

Picture how you would like to wear your ring: on its own, beside an engagement ring or as part of a small stack. On its own, you can focus on the pattern you enjoy. Beside another ring, I would give the fit of the pair as much attention as the look.

Either style can be chosen as a wedding ring. If you are still deciding whether you want one ring or two, my [engagement ring vs wedding ring guide](/moissanite-guide/engagement-ring-vs-wedding-ring) is a useful place to begin.

Check the side profiles, any gap and where the rings touch. A full or half eternity label does not establish a flush fit, and the two examples above have not been presented as tested partners for a particular engagement ring. Our broader [guide to choosing a moissanite ring](/moissanite-guide/how-to-choose-a-moissanite-ring) covers the surrounding shape, setting and metal decisions.

## Before you choose

1. **Decide which pattern matters.** Would a plain section coming into view bother you, or do you prefer a plain underside?
2. **Compare the exact rings.** Look at stone size, coverage, side profile and the lower band, rather than the full or half label alone.
3. **Confirm fit and future options.** Ask about sizing and design-specific resizing before ordering, especially for a surprise gift.
4. **Check your intended pairing.** If wearing two rings together, confirm the fit of that actual pair.

You do not have to choose full eternity for the ring to mean something lasting. I would choose the circle of stones if that is the detail you love, or the half eternity design if a row above and plain band below feels more like you. There is room for either. Take your time with the fit, then choose what feels like yours.
