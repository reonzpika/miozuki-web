# How Much Does a Moissanite Ring Cost in NZ?

I like to start with a budget that feels comfortable, then look at what each ring includes. In the examples gathered for this guide, silver and silver-alloy moissanite rings were advertised from NZ$119 to NZ$999.99. Selected solid-gold rings ranged from NZ$1,899 in 9k yellow gold to NZ$4,676 in 18k yellow gold. The price of your ring will depend on the design and options you choose.

That is a wide range of prices for rings that all have moissanite in their name. To make sense of it, I look beyond the centre stone to the metal, setting and support included with the piece. Understanding those details can make choosing feel a little less overwhelming.

As Miozuki’s founder, I wanted this guide to offer a wider view of the choices available to New Zealand buyers. It includes examples from Cullen, Diamond Stars, Miozuki, Nura Lab and NZ Jewellers. I approach each one with the same questions: what is it made from, which stones are included, and what does the price cover? The comparison draws on published details and selected product options; it does not include hands-on testing of the rings.

## What changes the price of a moissanite ring?

### The base metal and gold carat

I start with the full material description. A gold-coloured finish on sterling silver does not make the ring solid gold, and a silver–palladium alloy has a different composition again. If the wording leaves you unsure, ask about the base metal and any plating.

For solid gold, look at the stated carat as well as the colour. Two yellow-gold rings can look similar in photographs while using different gold alloys. A price difference between 9k and 18k gold therefore needs to be considered alongside the material difference.

The design and amount of metal also contribute to the finished price. Once you understand the material, you can look at how it fits into the piece as a whole.

### The centre stone and any side stones

Next, I look for the gemstone name, shape and dimensions in millimetres, alongside the carat label. It also helps to check whether a weight description refers to the centre stone or to all the stones together. The same headline carat number can describe different arrangements.

Where a design offers both moissanite and lab-grown diamond, make sure the price belongs to your chosen gemstone. Current product options may differ from those shown in an older search result.

With pavé, halo and three-stone designs, read the smaller-stone details as well. A ring described as a moissanite ring may have a moissanite centre and accents made from another material.

### Design, custom changes and support

A plain solitaire and a ring with several stone settings involve different design details. Custom changes can affect the price too, even when you begin with a standard style. If you have something particular in mind, ask for a quote that includes those changes.

I also leave room in the conversation for resizing, delivery and future maintenance. These can affect the overall cost, so read the terms for the provider and ring you are considering. If a warranty is included, check which work it covers and whether any conditions apply.

## How I would compare two quotes

When two rings catch your eye, put their details side by side. I find that clear answers to a few practical questions make the decision easier to follow.

| Check | What to ask |
|---|---|
| Currency and total | Is the price in NZD, and does the final total include tax and delivery to your address? |
| Selected option | Does the price apply to your chosen size, metal and stone, or is it an advertised starting price? |
| Material | What is the base metal? Is it plated? If it is gold, what carat is specified? |
| Centre stone | Is it moissanite? What shape and millimetre dimensions are listed? |
| Other stones | Are the smaller stones made from moissanite, zirconia, diamond or another material? |
| Size changes | Can this exact design be resized, and what would that cost? |
| Timing | Is it in stock or made to order? Does the stated timing include delivery? |
| Support | What do the current return and warranty terms cover for this order? |

Photographs and videos are helpful for exploring the look of a ring. For gemstone grades and measurements, turn to the written specifications, and ask about fit and comfort if you are unsure. I would want those questions answered before placing an order.

## Choosing a budget that feels comfortable

For me, the starting point is an amount you can spend comfortably. Then think about what you would most enjoy wearing: a particular metal, a simple band, a favourite stone shape or a little more detail. Those preferences can help you narrow the choices without letting the price make the whole decision.

If you love a plain band, you can keep the focus there, even when another design offers more stones. If gold matters to you, take time to compare the alloy and setting. A higher total does not, on its own, tell you more about the moissanite.

If a ring is beyond your budget, it is reasonable to reconsider the material or design, or simply take more time. Its meaning does not depend on reaching a particular figure. I’d want you to feel comfortable with the choice as well as enjoy the ring itself.

If you plan to buy a wedding band as well, consider the combined budget before choosing the first piece. The [engagement ring vs wedding ring guide](/moissanite-guide/engagement-ring-vs-wedding-ring) explains their different roles and buying order if you are still deciding whether you want one ring or two.
