## What changes the price of a pearl necklace?

### The pearls themselves

I would look closely at the reflections on the pearls before getting caught up in a size or grade label. Clear, bright reflections are one sign of strong lustre. [GIA identifies seven pearl value factors](https://www.gia.edu/pearl-quality-factor): size, shape, colour, lustre, surface, nacre and matching. Within the same pearl type, larger pearls generally carry more value when the other factors are equal. Roundness can also add value, although well-formed irregular shapes can be prized too.

That does not mean you need to choose round pearls. If you love a baroque outline, its character may matter more to you than uniformity. Ask for close-up photographs and measurements so you can understand the piece you are considering. If a seller uses a letter grade, ask what it describes before comparing it with another seller's label. Our [pearl buying guide](/pearl-guide) gives the wider background on pearl types and quality.

### The strand, chain and clasp

A strand brings a group of pearls together around the neck, while a pendant concentrates the detail in one pearl or a smaller group. Pearl selection, the number of strands, chain and clasp all contribute to the finished piece. If hand-knotting matters to you, look for an explicit description rather than assuming every strand is knotted between each pearl.

Length deserves a closer look too. In the examples above, one necklace measures 40 cm plus a 6 cm extension; another is 45 cm including its extension. Those descriptions do not mean the same thing. Check whether the stated length includes the clasp and extension before treating two necklaces as the same length.

### The metal and its finish

Sterling silver, stainless steel, gold-plated silver and 9ct gold are different materials. A white-gold finish on silver does not mean the chain is solid white gold. On a strand, a metal description may refer to the clasp and fittings rather than the whole necklace.

The gold-chain examples in this guide sit in a higher price range, but their pearls and construction differ as well. The published details do not let us separate out exactly how much of the price difference comes from the metal, pearls or other costs.

## Compare the whole necklace, not just the pearl size

It is tempting to compare two necklaces by their largest pearl. I find the full design more helpful. A single drop pearl leaves most of the chain visible; a strand creates a continuous line; two layers of smaller pearls give a different effect again.

A larger individual pearl does not automatically mean a more expensive necklace. These pieces have different amounts of pearl coverage and different construction. As you browse [pearl necklaces](/collections/pearl-necklace-nz), compare the overall design alongside the measurements, rather than expecting one number to explain the price.

## How I would choose a comfortable budget

Start with two necklaces you can picture yourself wearing, preferably in a similar style. For a pendant, consider the pearl and chain together. For a strand, look at how the pearls work as a group, including any deliberate variation in shape or size.

If you decide to spend more, be clear about what you want that extra spending to give you. It might be a different metal, a particular pearl shape, another strand or simply a design you enjoy more. When the description leaves you unsure, ask the seller for the missing detail. A higher price does not fill that gap for you.

For me, a useful budget is one that feels comfortable after you have understood the whole purchase. There is no need to reach the top of a price range to choose something meaningful.

## What to check before you order

Before placing an order, I would want clear answers to these questions:

- **What exactly is included?** Check whether the price covers a complete necklace, a pendant without a chain, or a pearl charm alone.
- **Which pearls and metals are listed?** Look for freshwater pearl descriptions, measurements, shape, disclosed treatments, and the underlying chain or clasp metal as well as any plating.
- **How is the length measured?** Separate the base length from any extension, and check whether the total includes the clasp.
- **What is the full delivered cost?** Confirm delivery charges and the timing for your chosen piece, particularly if it is made to order.
- **What support applies?** Read the seller's return terms. For a strand, you can also ask about restringing options and the cost if it is needed later.

For looking after the necklace once it arrives, keep our [pearl care guide](/pearl-guide/pearl-care) handy. If the pearl description is unclear, ask the seller about the pearl type and any treatments.
