# Moissanite Ring Certificates: What Does a GRA Report Tell You?

<GuideSchema title="Moissanite Ring Certificates: What Does a GRA Report Tell You?" path="/moissanite-guide/moissanite-ring-certificates-gra" updated="2026-11-09" />

A GRA report records details about a moissanite, such as its measurements, colour and clarity. To understand those grades, it helps to know who assigned them and how the stone was assessed.

Suppliers may use different standards to decide a moissanite's colour, clarity and cut grades. Two moissanite rings labelled D colour and VVS clarity have similar descriptions. The labels alone do not show whether they were judged in the same way.

A certificate can help you understand what is being offered. I would read it alongside the ring's photographs and product details, then ask who graded the stone and how. Here is what those descriptions mean in everyday language.

## Why can moissanite grades differ between suppliers?

Moissanite grading does not follow one system used by every manufacturer. A maker may grade its own stones, a retailer may use the maker's description, or a separate organisation may issue a report.

They may use familiar terms from diamond grading, such as D colour or VVS clarity. That shared wording does not tell you whether they used the same lighting, magnification or comparison stones to reach their decision.

Think of the grade as a description that needs a name behind it. You want to know who assigned it and what they looked for. A manufacturer can have a careful grading process of its own. Ask the seller to explain it in terms you can understand.

## What do the letters and numbers mean?

| Term | Plain-English meaning | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| D colour | A colourless description borrowed from diamond grading | Who decided the stone was D colour? |
| DEF colour | A range covering D, E and F colour descriptions | Does this describe your stone or the range generally? |
| VVS clarity | A description used for inclusions that are very difficult to see under magnification | How was the stone examined? |
| Excellent or ideal cut | A description of how well the stone has been cut | What does that term mean for this supplier? |
| Carat | A weight figure, or sometimes a diamond-equivalent size description | What are the stone's actual millimetre measurements? |

The descriptions cover different features. D colour tells you about colour, while VVS tells you about clarity. Neither describes the ring's metal or the quality of its setting.

### D and DEF colour

D is a single colour grade. DEF is a range of three grades, D, E and F. A listing that says DEF does not promise that every stone has been individually graded D.

These letters come from GIA's diamond colour scale, which starts at D for colourless. When a moissanite supplier uses them, ask how it compares and grades its own stones. Using GIA's terminology does not mean GIA graded the moissanite. [GIA colour scale](https://www.gia.edu/gia-about/4cs-color)

For example, a seller may describe its whole range as DEF. If a specific colour grade matters to you, ask whether the description is for your chosen stone or the range as a whole.

I would also look at a clear video in ordinary light. Lighting, camera settings and the surrounding metal can affect how colour looks on a screen, so use the video to see the appearance you enjoy alongside the written description.

### VVS clarity

Clarity describes features inside or on the surface of a stone. Internal features are called inclusions.

VVS stands for very, very slightly included. In GIA's diamond system, VVS1 and VVS2 describe inclusions that are difficult for a skilled grader to see at 10× magnification. That means looking at the stone enlarged ten times. A moissanite seller using VVS should be able to explain how that description was decided. [GIA clarity scale](https://www.gia.edu/gia-about/4cs-clarity)

VVS does not mean the stone has no inclusions. It also says nothing about its colour. A stone can have a high clarity description and still show a tint.

For everyday wear, a helpful question is whether any inclusions can be seen without magnification. Sellers sometimes call this eye-clean. Ask what they mean by that, since eyesight and viewing distance affect what someone can see.

### Excellent or ideal cut

Cut describes how a stone has been shaped and finished. Its proportions and the arrangement of its flat surfaces, called facets, influence how it looks in light.

Excellent and ideal can sound like clear rankings, but you still need to know how the supplier uses those terms. Ask whether it follows a defined grading method or uses the word as a product description.

A short video of the stone moving can help you see how it looks from different angles. If you are comparing the surrounding designs too, our [solitaire, pavé and halo guide](/moissanite-guide/solitaire-vs-pave-vs-halo-moissanite-rings) shows where each style places the detail.

## Does GIA grade the moissanite in my ring?

D colour and VVS clarity on a listing do not mean GIA examined your stone. GIA is the Gemological Institute of America, which developed the diamond scales behind those terms.

There are also two different questions a laboratory can answer. Identification asks what the stone is. Grading describes qualities such as colour and clarity. GIA has identified moissanite in its research without assigning it the diamond clarity grade used for a diamond report. [GIA's examination of moissanite](https://www.gia.edu/gems-gemology/fall-2020-labnotes-synthetic-moissanite-fraudulent-gia-inscription)

Independent grading arrangements have been announced elsewhere. IGI, the International Gemological Institute, and manufacturer Charles & Colvard announced moissanite grading reports for that manufacturer's stones. The practical point is to check the actual organisation and report, since grading services and standards can differ. [IGI partnership announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/charles--colvard-forges-exclusive-strategic-alliance-with-igi-to-supply-moissanite-grading-reports-302154267.html)

## What does a GRA report tell me?

A GRA report may list the stone's shape, measurements, colour and clarity, along with a report number. It gives you a written record of those descriptions.

GRA and GIA are different names. Read the full name of the organisation on the document and ask the seller three questions.

1. Who graded the stone?
2. Was this particular stone examined?
3. How do the report details connect to the stone in this ring?

If the report number works online, compare the online details with the card and product listing. A matching record is useful, but the lookup does not explain how the grades were decided.

Also ask about anything the report leaves out. For example, the Gemmological Association of Australia examined surface-coated moissanites whose GRA reports omitted the coating. That finding concerns those samples. If you are buying a coloured stone, ask how its colour was produced and whether it needs particular care. [GAA study of coated moissanite](https://www.gem.org.au/ag-article/surface-coated-synthetic-moissanite-now-widely-available/)

## How would I compare two rings with the same grade?

Imagine two rings both described as D colour and VVS clarity. One has a larger centre stone in silver. The other has a smaller centre stone in gold.

The matching grades tell you how colour and clarity are being described. They do not explain the difference in size, metal, setting or price. You would still need to compare those details and ask who assigned each grade.

The same applies to two similar-looking rings from different suppliers. One may come with an individual stone report, while the other uses a general description for the product range. Ask what the information applies to before deciding which ring suits you.

My approach would be to bring four things together.

- **The grading explanation.** Who assigned the grades, and what do those grades mean?
- **The dimensions.** How large is the centre stone in millimetres?
- **The appearance.** Can you see the stone and setting clearly in photographs or video?
- **The materials.** What metal, finish and accent stones are included in your selected option?

Our [moissanite ring size guide](/moissanite-guide/moissanite-ring-sizes-1-to-5-carats) helps with measurements, and the [sterling silver and plating guide](/moissanite-guide/sterling-silver-moissanite-rings) explains metal descriptions.

## Choosing your moissanite ring

A grade is most helpful when you understand who assigned it and what it describes. From there, you can give your attention to the ring's appearance, materials and size.

If something is unclear, a simple question is enough to begin. “Could you explain who graded this stone and whether the description applies to the particular ring I am choosing?”
